Industry-first reverse search · 27 October 2026

A substantive survey should be anchored in a live industrial research problem, not simply in a convenient tutorial. We examined named, public sources from Amazon Science, Google Research/YouTube, Microsoft Research, Spotify, Meta, eBay and Netflix. This is a source-backed audit of public work; it is not a claim to have indexed every private laboratory seminar.

One question worth submitting today

When Can Offline Ranking Evaluation Be Trusted? Assumptions and Failure Modes in Counterfactual Learning-to-Rank (2015–2026)

Why industry cares: rolling out a new ranking policy costs time, money and possible user harm. Logged interactions allow cheap tests, but only under statistical and behavioral assumptions. Candidate selection, position and trust bias, item interactions, business-rule reordering, insufficient policy overlap and delayed rewards can break these assumptions.

The original synthesis: connect five failure types into one comparative framework: (1) missing/confounded feedback; (2) biased click models; (3) estimator bias–variance and deficient support; (4) changes to the candidate set or displayed ranking; (5) sequential or long-term reward. Ask precisely what each paper estimates, what assumptions it needs, and what empirical evidence tests those assumptions.

Not an implementation project. Read the mathematics, proofs and published experiments; no new experiments required by the professor.

8. Trace the evolution of each research domain

Use this ST100 pattern: problem → first formal model → key estimator or algorithm → known failure → improved theory → modern applications. These are starting anchors, not a falsely complete bibliography of 15 papers. For the final survey, expand each chain through its tutorial's references and verify the original papers.

A. Unbiased LTR — the strongest chronological spine

  1. 2015 · Counterfactual learning: move from logged-policy outcomes to estimators for unseen target decisions (see the KDD 2022 foundations).
  2. 2017 · Debias clicked rankings: Joachims, Swaminathan and Schnabel, Unbiased Learning-to-Rank with Biased Feedback (WSDM). Formalize exposure propensity and a counterfactual ranking objective.
  3. 2018–2021 · Address assumptions and instability: propensity inference without randomized interventions, selection bias, trust bias, large variance and learning objectives. Use the SIGIR 2023 tutorial bibliography to select exact papers.
  4. 2022 · Estimator comparisons: the KDD tutorial explicitly compares direct method, IPS, self-normalized IPS, doubly robust, Switch and shrinkage estimators.
  5. 2023–2024 · Broader bias corrections: WSDM 2024 ULTR tutorial surveys new estimators, applications and fairness.
  6. 2025 · Reproducibility/robustness: SIGIR 2025 robustness study tests sensitivity of counterfactual ranking models to logging and simulated-feedback assumptions.

Mathematical questions for viva: what probability goes in an inverse-propensity denominator? Under what logging assumptions is the estimator unbiased? Why does importance weighting increase variance? What does a doubly robust estimator require? Can it be unbiased with a misspecified click model?

B. DPP diversity — a mathematical spine

  1. 2012 · Probabilistic model of diversity: Kulesza & Taskar, DPPs for Machine Learning introduces determinant-based set probabilities, sampling and inference.
  2. 2010s · Algorithmic scaling: trace practical greedy MAP inference and personalized recommendation from the monograph's citing papers. Check complexity and approximation quality.
  3. 2020s · Diverse search/recommendation: use Result Diversification in Search and Recommendation (2024 survey) to compare metrics and alternatives.
  4. Alternative theory: ETH submodularity bibliography and Stanford notes motivate greedily approximating diminishing-returns objectives.

Viva questions: why does a determinant reward diversity? What is the quality-similarity kernel? How do you maximize the set probability? Is a DPP objective always equivalent to a submodular one? What assumptions give approximation guarantees?

C. Exposure fairness — a mature alternate spine

  1. 2017–2018: develop constrained fair rankings and Singh & Joachims, Fairness of Exposure in Rankings (KDD 2018).
  2. 2018: Biega et al., Equity of Attention formalizes amortized individual fairness over sequences of rankings.
  3. 2022: Ekstrand et al., Fairness in Information Access Systems surveys the taxonomy across stakeholders.
  4. 2025–2026: Economic fairness tutorial series adds market, supply/demand and long-term dynamics, with PDFs from SIGIR 2025, WWW 2026, and ECIR/ICMR 2026.

Viva questions: how is exposure measured? What fairness constraint is enforced, and for whom? Why does optimizing individual accuracy conflict with provider exposure? What changes when users and producers adapt over time?

D. Contextual bandits — from classic exploration to recommendation

  1. Classics: confidence-bound and Thompson-style exploration; ICML 2022 bibliography links Auer 2002 and linear contextual bandit papers.
  2. 2022: ICML tutorial organizes statistical complexity and regret, with video, slides and paper references.
  3. 2024: WWW tutorial slides specialize the model to personalized recommendation.
  4. 2025: RecSys Bandits in the Wild brings delayed feedback and exploration policy constraints.

Viva questions: what is regret and how is it bounded? What context is known before acting? Which exploration policy is safe? How is an off-policy value estimated from past recommendations?

10. A practical way to finish the survey

  1. Choose one topic and write a one-paragraph research question.
