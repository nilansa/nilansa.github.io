Initial recommendation

First choice to investigate: diversity-aware recommendation using Determinantal Point Processes (DPPs), compared with submodular diversification. DPPs are an explicit example from the professor, have a clear mathematical core, and appear less directly covered than basic ranking and retrieval topics.

Backup: unbiased learning-to-rank from biased feedback. It has the strongest verified tutorial package: a WSDM 2024 tutorial, slides and a recording. To go beyond the course, focus on propensity estimation, confounding, counterfactual risk, estimator variance, robustness and fairness—not only basic position bias.

1. What the professor expects

  • One non-trivial topic related to the course, but beyond the lecture material.
  • About 10–15 connected research papers, including a couple of seminal papers that shaped the field.
  • Mathematics, theory, algorithms, assumptions, limitations and published empirical results.
  • A short report and presentation, followed by an oral viva. Do not plan new experiments or a systems implementation; compare published results instead.

6. A practical way to finish the survey

  1. Choose one topic and write a one-paragraph research question.
  2. Use a tutorial to create a taxonomy. Identify two seminal papers and a timeline of 10–15 connected papers.
  3. Read the papers, compare assumptions and mathematical objectives, and record where each method improves or fails.
  4. Prepare the report and viva around the connections between papers, not around isolated summaries.

Search rule: prefer official conference, university or research-lab pages. A paper mentioning a topic is not evidence that a tutorial or course exists. Mark material as “not verified” if no direct slides or recording can be found.
