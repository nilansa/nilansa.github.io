Answer in one minute

Yes, a well-scoped topic can qualify; no, a generic title alone does not. The assignment does not require proposing a new research result. It does require independently connecting difficult mathematical papers, not copying a survey's chapter structure.

First choice for initial submission: “When Does Counterfactual Ranking Evaluation Fail? Support Violations, Click-Model Misspecification and Display-Policy Post-Processing.” This is a narrow lens on the professor's unbiased-learning-to-rank example, rather than a new unrelated topic.

Alternatives: exposure allocation in ranking (the strongest distinct independent topic), DPP kernels and log-determinant top-k diversity (explicit professor example), and off-policy evaluation of contextual bandit recommendation policies (explicit professor example).

Crucial limit: these are credible initial proposals, not pre-approved survey submissions. The exact final 10–15-paper bibliography and the student's ability to derive the key equations still need checking. 3. Four genuine contenders

A · Counterfactual ranking estimator failures

Hard gates: likely pass all, once the bibliography is pruned and the scope stays within the same statistical question.

Math: policy value, inverse propensity weighting, support/overlap, bias–variance, click-model misspecification, displayed-list mismatch.

Industry: Amazon 2023 business rules → SIGIR 2024 flexible click models → RecSys 2026 position weights.

Learning: KDD 2022 three-hour tutorial plus WSDM 2024 ULTR.

Boundary: avoid full offline reinforcement learning, candidate-generation systems and long-term welfare in the core report.

B · DPP-based top-k diversity

Hard gates: likely pass with a precise kernel/inference comparison and 10–15 genuinely relevant DPP/recommendation papers.

Math: determinant probability, PSD kernels, sampling, MAP inference and the non-monotone submodularity of log-determinants. Do not present DPP and submodular optimization as mutually exclusive.

Sources: Kulesza–Taskar 2012 monograph and 2024 Microsoft Research-linked diversification survey.

Risk: fewer verified recent DPP-specific industry tutorials; mathematically demanding reconstruction from original papers.

C · Fair exposure across repeated rankings

Hard gates: likely pass if restricted to one fairness measure and its mathematical optimization.

Math: exposure as position-dependent attention, repeated allocations, utility/fairness constraints, stochastic rankings.

Sources: KDD 2018 Singh–Joachims, ECIR 2025 fairness tutorial, and 2025–2026 economic tutorial decks.

Risk: an entire review of social fairness, regulation, feedback loops and welfare is too broad.

D · Contextual bandit OPE for recommendation

Hard gates: credible after excluding generic all-of-RL coverage and confirming no major overlap with later IRE lectures.

Math: policy regret, exploration, importance weighting, off-policy evaluation and action support.

Sources: ICML 2022 tutorial with video/slides/papers, WSDM 2024 bandits tutorial, RecSys 2025 tutorial.

Risk: overlaps topic A unless the target question is policy exploration rather than ranking-click estimation.

4. Mathematical ST100 example: why is topic A non-trivial?

Suppose an old recommender shows item A, and your proposed policy would show B. Historical logs record the outcome for A, not what the same user would have done with B. This is a counterfactual—an outcome from an action we did not actually take.

Estimated value of new policy
 = average over logged cases [
     reward × P(new policy chose logged action)
              / P(old logging policy chose that action)
   ]

For that estimate to be valid, the old logging policy must have a positive chance of displaying every action the new policy might choose (support/overlap), the importance weights must be correctly specified, and the observed reward must reflect the assumed feedback process. Clicks can violate the last assumption because placement and trust alter click probability. Business rules can violate the action description because users see a reordered list, not the raw model output.

Questions that reveal deep understanding: What happens if the denominator is zero? Can any logged-data estimator recover unseen outcomes without further assumptions? Why can an unbiased estimator still be unusable because of variance? Which click-model assumptions does PBM make? How do an item-position model, INTERPOL and GPBM trade modelling assumptions for variance? How can business-rule post-processing change the propensity of the actual displayed ranking?

6. Copy-ready initial proposal and decision checklist

Proposed title: When Does Counterfactual Ranking Evaluation Fail? Support Violations, Click-Model Misspecification and Display-Policy Post-Processing.

Initial proposal: I propose a theory-focused survey of the assumptions that make offline evaluation of ranking policies possible using historical user interactions. I will examine three linked failure modes: insufficient overlap between logging and target ranking policies; incorrect models of examination and click behaviour; and differences between the ranker's generated list and the list actually displayed after post-processing. Using approximately 10–15 connected papers spanning foundational counterfactual risk minimization and modern learning-to-rank research (2015–2026), I will compare IPS-based and more recent estimators, their mathematical guarantees, bias–variance trade-offs and published empirical evidence. The focus will be on explaining when estimates are identifiable and how methods address assumptions that earlier work made; I will not run new experiments or implement a ranking system. The research question will be finalized with the instructor's feedback.
