Demo principle

Show a decision. Do not spend the four minutes filling an empty form. Use a prepared, clearly labelled illustrative condition scenario on the real anonymised source record NH-083. Rehearse the clicks before presenting and show the computed output, not unsupported model claims.

Five-minute setup before judges enter

1

Launch the unchanged submitted app

From the archived repo clone, run node scripts/server.mjs, then open http://127.0.0.1:4173. Confirm that My Home and Portfolio work without Ollama. If the local LLM is available, the status should report the selected model; if not, keep the rules fallback ready.

2

Find a better case through Portfolio search

Search NH-083 in Portfolio and open it in My Home. It is not among the first 24 landing-page sample choices. The source row has EPC E, London, 5,700 kWh electricity and 24,700 kWh gas.

3

Set illustrative, explicitly unverified building conditions

Expand “Heating, fabric and controls”: select gas boiler, poor insulation, single glazing, smart controls No, solar panels installed No. Expand “Checks for a more specific plan”: roof sound, accessible loft present Yes. These inputs represent hypothetical owner confirmations for demonstration, not facts present in NEED.

4

Move to Assessment, then Action plan

The revised score should be 43/100 (Poor, still provisional because the area is a band midpoint). Loft and smart controls should be feasible. At Stage 3, set a £2,000 budget and the objective of reducing bills; compare loft plus controls with doing nothing.

5

Prepare one safe live question

Use the explanation field with: “For this package, show electricity, gas, carbon savings and the assumptions.” If the local model is unavailable, the rules selector still uses engine-backed fact templates. Do not force a live model installation during the pitch.

Important: the demo's building-condition values are scenario confirmations, not independently verified home facts. Tell the audience that once. Do not represent the predicted costs, comfort effects, EPC improvements or values as observed outcomes.

Full four-minute spoken script

Target: about 3:50–3:55 including short clicks. Bracketed lines are stage directions, not spoken. Rehearse the narration aloud on the actual computer; UI latency can vary.

1. The problem and promise

0:00–0:24 · 24 s

“A homeowner knows their energy bill, but usually does not know which upgrades are sensible. A heat pump might help, or it might be premature. GreenHome brings evidence, building constraints and costs into one decision: what can this home improve, what might it save, and what must be checked first?”

Show: My Home with NH-083 already selected. No opening animations or login story.

2. Data first, assumptions labelled

0:24–0:52 · 28 s

“This example comes from a real, anonymised UK government NEED record—not synthetic customer data. It reports an EPC-E home, 5,700 kilowatt-hours of electricity and 24,700 of gas per year. Roof condition and insulation quality are not in that dataset. For this demonstration, I have entered hypothetical homeowner confirmations, and the system identifies what remains uncertain.”

Show: source labels on Stage 1, then click “Continue to assessment”.

3. Explain the score

0:52–1:20 · 28 s

“The Green Home Score is transparent: seven required components, with fixed weights. It is computed by code, not guessed by an LLM. Here the score is 43 out of 100: Poor. It remains provisional because the floor area is only a band midpoint. We show each component, its evidence and its contribution.”

Show: 43/100 and briefly expand “contribution details”. Avoid reading every weight aloud.

4. Feasibility before recommendations

1:20–1:49 · 29 s

“This next section is the difference between generic tips and useful advice. Each measure is feasible, conditional, or unsuitable. A leaking roof would block loft insulation and rooftop solar. Unknown planning or electrical readiness stays conditional. The system does not invent missing checks, and conditional upgrades are not automatically treated as approved.”

Show: upgrade status list. Point to one conditional or blocked status; then click “Continue to action plan”.

5. One business decision with numbers

1:49–2:29 · 40 s

“Now suppose the owner has a £2,000 limit and wants lower energy bills. The planner compares feasible combinations against doing nothing. In this example, loft insulation plus smart controls costs an indicative £1,450. The model estimates 4,140 kilowatt-hours less gas each year, about 755 kilograms less carbon, and £330 in annual variable-bill savings. These are documented scenario assumptions, not guaranteed savings or an installer quote.”

Show: budget and bill objective, then selected package versus no-work comparison. Highlight gas, cost and carbon.

6. What-if and bounded AI

2:29–3:04 · 35 s

“If we also consider a heat pump, gas demand falls but electricity can rise. We model the fuels separately and apply their separate carbon factors. For natural-language questions, our local Qwen model—or an offline fallback—selects supported facts. The numerical engine recalculates them first. The LLM cannot invent a new score, savings figure, grant decision or property value.”

Show: preferably briefly point to a heat-pump comparison, then ask the pretested explanation question. If Ollama is slow, proceed without waiting.

7. ML proof, not ML theatre

3:04–3:34 · 30 s

“Behind this, two compact gradient-boosting regressors estimate missing electricity and gas use from five home categories. They never overwrite a recorded reading. On held-out NEED data, they reduced average error by about two percent and six percent over a simple median baseline. Retrofit effects remain explicit calculations because we do not have observed outcomes for these homes.”

Show: status or record provenance, not a fake “AI accuracy” gauge.

8. Portfolio and final line

3:34–3:54 · 20 s

“Finally, Portfolio lets us screen one hundred anonymised examples by need and improvement potential. This is a prototype, not a production bank service. Our central principle is simple: recommend only what the evidence supports, explain every number, and tell the homeowner what to verify before spending money.”

Show: Portfolio filtering/search. End on this sentence. Do not add extra features beyond four minutes.

Failure-safe backup plan

Ollama unavailable

Keep the demo

The numerical engine and UI still work. State: “The local model is optional; the offline facts selector gives the same code-computed numbers.” Do not wait for downloads or restart services in front of judges.

Internet unavailable

Still functional offline

The 100 recorded demonstration homes, score, simulator and existing model artifacts are local. Third-party guidance links and installing a missing language model need connectivity.

Less than one minute left

Prioritise the comparison

Skip the photo, unrelated value form and verbose portfolio browsing. Show the chosen package, costs, fuel changes and assumptions, then deliver the final line.

Judge challenges a number

Show its chain

Observed 2024 source row → owner-confirmed conditions → deterministic model assumption → per-fuel difference → documented factor. If input is unknown, say it is unknown.
