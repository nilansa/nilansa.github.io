Ranker cascades and budgets

Cheap retrieval and inexpensive first-pass scoring shrink the candidate set before expensive reranking.

CoreProvided module reference

Basis: M3 p.21; M2 p.32. Page labels point to the provided files or named reading; the explanation and exercises here are independently written.

01 / Understand

What actually happens?

A multi-stage service manages recall, feature fetching and p99. An expensive cross-encoder on every corpus document is impossible, but reranking tens or hundreds is feasible. Monitor stage latency and the possibility of missing good results early.

Core principle
Cheap retrieval and inexpensive first-pass scoring shrink the candidate set before expensive reranking.

Try to reason through the mechanism

  1. Identify what the system is trying to preserve or measure in the situation.
  2. Separate the observed symptom from the cause described in this lesson.
  3. Choose the operation, model or trade-off that fits that cause; name its failure mode.
02 / Small example

A concrete case

Retrieve 1000 candidates, score quickly to 100, apply a heavy model to 20 and return 10.

Equation or decision rule

Total serving time ≈ stage costs + fan-out/merge

Know what each symbol or action means. The instructor's announced final-exam format emphasizes applying principles to short objective scenarios.

03 / Examiner's trap

A tempting answer that fails

Increasing the heavy ranker's candidate count without checking latency or coverage.

Use an explicit four-part answer: symptom → principle → mechanism → consequence. A strong answer explains why the apparently easy choice is unsafe or incomplete.

04 / Active recall

Exam-style scenario

A new reranker doubles quality on judged pairs but breaks p99. What's the proper response?
One-line viva / short-answer question

What is the irrecoverable error in a ranking cascade?

A relevant item eliminated before the later scorer can see it.
