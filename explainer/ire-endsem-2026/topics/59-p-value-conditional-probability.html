Lesson 59 / 69 · A/B testing

P-value: conditional probability

A p-value measures how surprising the observed test statistic or more extreme outcomes would be assuming the null model.

High yieldInstructor-directed reading

Basis: Goodman (2008) pp.135–136; Anil's linked reading. Page labels point to the provided files or named reading; the explanation and exercises here are independently written.

01 / Understand

What actually happens?

It is not the probability that the null is true, that the treatment works, or that a launched product fails. Interpret it with design assumptions, effect size, confidence intervals, prior evidence and operational risks. A small p-value is evidence against the null model, not an effect-size estimate.

Core principle
A p-value measures how surprising the observed test statistic or more extreme outcomes would be assuming the null model.

Try to reason through the mechanism

  1. Identify what the system is trying to preserve or measure in the situation.
  2. Separate the observed symptom from the cause described in this lesson.
  3. Choose the operation, model or trade-off that fits that cause; name its failure mode.
02 / Small example

A concrete case

p=0.03 is not a 97% probability that the new ranking wins.

Equation or decision rule

p = P(statistic as/extreme | H0, model)

Know what each symbol or action means. The instructor's announced final-exam format emphasizes applying principles to short objective scenarios.

03 / Examiner's trap

A tempting answer that fails

Reading p=0.03 as a 3% chance the null is true.

Use an explicit four-part answer: symptom → principle → mechanism → consequence. A strong answer explains why the apparently easy choice is unsafe or incomplete.

04 / Active recall

Exam-style scenario

A test reports p=0.03. Which interpretation is correct?
One-line viva / short-answer question

What must be assumed in computing a p-value?

A specified null statistical model and its sampling/test assumptions.
