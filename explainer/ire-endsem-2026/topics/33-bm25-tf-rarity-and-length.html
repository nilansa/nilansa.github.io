BM25: TF, rarity and length

BM25 combines corpus rarity, saturated term matches, and length normalization.

High yieldProvided module reference

Basis: M3 pp.10–11. Page labels point to the provided files or named reading; the explanation and exercises here are independently written.

01 / Understand

What actually happens?

Each query term contributes IDF times a saturating TF fraction. The denominator grows with doc length relative to average length when b>0. k1 controls TF saturation; b controls the length penalty. Both are tunable, not universal constants.

Core principle
BM25 combines corpus rarity, saturated term matches, and length normalization.

02 / Small example

A concrete case

A tightly focused product page can outrank a bloated keyword-stuffed page despite lower raw tf.

Equation or decision rule

BM25=Σ idf(t)·tf(k1+1)/(tf+k1(1−b+b·dl/avgdl))

03 / Examiner's trap

A tempting answer that fails

Interpreting b as a relevance threshold rather than length-normalization strength.

Use an explicit four-part answer: symptom → principle → mechanism → consequence. A strong answer explains why the apparently easy choice is unsafe or incomplete.

04 / Active recall

Exam-style scenario

What does setting b=0 do in BM25?
One-line viva / short-answer question

Explain roles of k1 and b in one sentence.

k1 sets term-frequency saturation; b determines how strongly document length affects the denominator.
