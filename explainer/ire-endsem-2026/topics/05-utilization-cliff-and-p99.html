Utilization cliff and p99

Queueing delay grows nonlinearly near saturation; the tail suffers first.

High yieldProvided module reference

Basis: M1 p.36; Quiz-2 case 1. Page labels point to the provided files or named reading; the explanation and exercises here are independently written.

01 / Understand

What actually happens?

With mean service time S and utilization U, the simple queueing approximation R ≈ S/(1−U) illustrates why spare capacity is required for bursts. A system at 80% busy is not safely '20% free'.

Core principle
Queueing delay grows nonlinearly near saturation; the tail suffers first.

Try to reason through the mechanism

  1. Identify what the system is trying to preserve or measure in the situation.
  2. Separate the observed symptom from the cause described in this lesson.
  3. Choose the operation, model or trade-off that fits that cause; name its failure mode.
02 / Small example

A concrete case

An API with stable median and doubled p99 at 80% CPU needs added capacity or lower load, not reassurance.

Equation or decision rule

R ≈ S/(1−U); serving target often U ≲ 0.7

Know what each symbol or action means. The instructor's announced final-exam format emphasizes applying principles to short objective scenarios.

03 / Examiner's trap

A tempting answer that fails

Interpreting unused average CPU as guaranteed low tail latency.

Use an explicit four-part answer: symptom → principle → mechanism → consequence. A strong answer explains why the apparently easy choice is unsafe or incomplete.

04 / Active recall

Exam-style scenario

Median latency is steady but p99 doubles when CPU reaches 80%. Best response?
One-line viva / short-answer question

Why reserve headroom for live serving?

Headroom absorbs bursts so queuing delay and p99 do not explode near saturation.
