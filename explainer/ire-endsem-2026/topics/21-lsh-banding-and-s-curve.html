LSH banding and S-curve

Banding makes candidate selection probable rather than exhaustive.

High yieldProvided module reference

Basis: M2 pp.15–16; MidSem key p.3. Page labels point to the provided files or named reading; the explanation and exercises here are independently written.

01 / Understand

What actually happens?

Split k=br signature coordinates into b bands of r rows. A candidate requires equality of all rows within at least one band. More rows make the method stricter; more bands make it looser; exact verification comes afterward.

Core principle
Banding makes candidate selection probable rather than exhaustive.

Try to reason through the mechanism

  1. Identify what the system is trying to preserve or measure in the situation.
  2. Separate the observed symptom from the cause described in this lesson.
  3. Choose the operation, model or trade-off that fits that cause; name its failure mode.
02 / Small example

A concrete case

For b=20,r=5, even a similar pair may be missed; unrelated collisions still require verification.

Equation or decision rule

P(candidate|s)=1−(1−s^r)^b; threshold roughly (1/b)^(1/r)

Know what each symbol or action means. The instructor's announced final-exam format emphasizes applying principles to short objective scenarios.

03 / Examiner's trap

A tempting answer that fails

Assuming candidate selection is itself exact duplicate classification.

Use an explicit four-part answer: symptom → principle → mechanism → consequence. A strong answer explains why the apparently easy choice is unsafe or incomplete.

04 / Active recall

Exam-style scenario

Missing moderately similar pairs is much worse than extra verification. With fixed k, what change helps recall?
One-line viva / short-answer question

What does an LSH false positive mean?

A dissimilar pair enters the candidate set and wastes exact-verification work.
