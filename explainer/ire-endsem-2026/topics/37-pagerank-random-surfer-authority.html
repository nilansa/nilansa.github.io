PageRank random-surfer authority

Incoming links confer authority in proportion to the linking pages' own authority.

High yieldProvided module reference

Basis: M3 p.14. Page labels point to the provided files or named reading; the explanation and exercises here are independently written.

01 / Understand

What actually happens?

A random surfer follows an outgoing link with probability d and teleports otherwise. Iterative stationary scores are computed offline across the crawl graph and stored as one per-document feature for fast serving.

Core principle
Incoming links confer authority in proportion to the linking pages' own authority.

Try to reason through the mechanism

  1. Identify what the system is trying to preserve or measure in the situation.
  2. Separate the observed symptom from the cause described in this lesson.
  3. Choose the operation, model or trade-off that fits that cause; name its failure mode.
02 / Small example

A concrete case

A small page linked by several trusted pages can outrank a keyword-stuffed page with no reputable links.

Equation or decision rule

PR(p)=(1−d)/N + d·Σ_{u→p} PR(u)/outdegree(u)

Know what each symbol or action means. The instructor's announced final-exam format emphasizes applying principles to short objective scenarios.

03 / Examiner's trap

A tempting answer that fails

Counting every backlink as the same vote regardless of its source.

Use an explicit four-part answer: symptom → principle → mechanism → consequence. A strong answer explains why the apparently easy choice is unsafe or incomplete.

One-line viva / short-answer question
