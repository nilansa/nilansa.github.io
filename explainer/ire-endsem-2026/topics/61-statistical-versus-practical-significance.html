Lesson 61 / 69 · A/B testing

Statistical versus practical significance

Even highly statistically significant effects may be tiny or commercially irrelevant.

High yieldInstructor-directed reading

Basis: Goodman (2008) p.136; Anil's linked reading. Page labels point to the provided files or named reading; the explanation and exercises here are independently written.

01 / Understand

What actually happens?

With enormous samples, a 0.01% difference may be detected precisely. Product decisions should account for effect magnitude, user value, cost, fairness and operational risk. Conversely, an important effect in a small sample may be uncertain.

Core principle
Even highly statistically significant effects may be tiny or commercially irrelevant.

Try to reason through the mechanism

  1. Identify what the system is trying to preserve or measure in the situation.
  2. Separate the observed symptom from the cause described in this lesson.
  3. Choose the operation, model or trade-off that fits that cause; name its failure mode.
02 / Small example

A concrete case

A 0.001 percentage-point CTR lift at p=0.00001 is not automatically worth a costly ranker.

Equation or decision rule

effect size and p-value answer different questions

Know what each symbol or action means. The instructor's announced final-exam format emphasizes applying principles to short objective scenarios.

03 / Examiner's trap

A tempting answer that fails

Assuming a smaller p-value means a larger benefit.

Use an explicit four-part answer: symptom → principle → mechanism → consequence. A strong answer explains why the apparently easy choice is unsafe or incomplete.

04 / Active recall

Exam-style scenario

A latency-heavy model gives a trivial CTR gain with p<0.001. What matters for launch?
One-line viva / short-answer question

Why can huge samples make tiny effects significant?

Standard errors shrink with sample size, so small deviations can be detected.
