Calibration and probability reliability

Among outcomes predicted with probability p, roughly p fraction should occur.

High yieldProvided module reference

Basis: M3 p.38. Page labels point to the provided files or named reading; the explanation and exercises here are independently written.

01 / Understand

What actually happens?

Ranking discrimination and probability accuracy are separate. A model predicting 0.4 for events occurring 0.2 of the time is overconfident. Platt/isotonic fitting on a held-out, representative set can help; downsampling negatives requires correction.

Core principle
Among outcomes predicted with probability p, roughly p fraction should occur.

Try to reason through the mechanism

  1. Identify what the system is trying to preserve or measure in the situation.
  2. Separate the observed symptom from the cause described in this lesson.
  3. Choose the operation, model or trade-off that fits that cause; name its failure mode.
02 / Small example

A concrete case

Two models have similar ROC-AUC but one consistently overstates click probabilities 2×; auction prices become wrong.

Equation or decision rule

calibration: E[y|p_hat≈p]≈p

Know what each symbol or action means. The instructor's announced final-exam format emphasizes applying principles to short objective scenarios.

03 / Examiner's trap

A tempting answer that fails

Assuming perfect AUC implies perfect probability calibration.

Use an explicit four-part answer: symptom → principle → mechanism → consequence. A strong answer explains why the apparently easy choice is unsafe or incomplete.

04 / Active recall

Exam-style scenario

When predictions cluster around 0.3 but observed clicks are near 0.1, what is wrong?
One-line viva / short-answer question

Why are sampled-negative CTR datasets a risk?

Training class prevalence differs from serving prevalence, distorting raw probabilities.
