Confidence intervals and estimation

An interval reports uncertainty in an estimated effect under repeated-sampling assumptions.

High yieldSyllabus-derived extension

Basis: End-sem announcement 14 Nov; A/B testing extension. Page labels point to the provided files or named reading; the explanation and exercises here are independently written.

01 / Understand

What actually happens?

A 95% confidence-interval procedure covers the fixed population parameter in 95% of hypothetical repeated experiments under the model; the observed interval is not literally a 95% probability distribution over the parameter. Use interval widths and decision thresholds, not just whether zero lies inside.

Core principle
An interval reports uncertainty in an estimated effect under repeated-sampling assumptions.

Try to reason through the mechanism

  1. Identify what the system is trying to preserve or measure in the situation.

02 / Small example

A concrete case

A treatment lift estimate +2% with CI [−1%, +5%] is uncertain about sign and magnitude.

03 / Examiner's trap

A tempting answer that fails

Interpreting a specific classical 95% CI as a 95% posterior probability without a Bayesian model.

Use an explicit four-part answer: symptom → principle → mechanism → consequence. A strong answer explains why the apparently easy choice is unsafe or incomplete.

Exam-style scenario

One-line viva / short-answer question

What is a confidence interval useful for beyond a p-value?

It shows estimated effect size and precision, including practically important ranges.
