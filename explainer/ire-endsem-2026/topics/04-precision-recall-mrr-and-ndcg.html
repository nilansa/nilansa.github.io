Lesson 04 / 69 · Systems & indexing

Precision, recall, MRR and nDCG

Metrics answer different questions about retrieved sets and order.

CoreProvided module reference

Basis: M1 p.17; M3 pp.18–19. Page labels point to the provided files or named reading; the explanation and exercises here are independently written.

01 / Understand

What actually happens?

Precision@K is relevant retrieved divided by K; Recall@K is relevant retrieved divided by all relevant; MRR rewards the first relevant rank; DCG discounts relevance by position, and nDCG normalizes by the best possible ordering.

Core principle
Metrics answer different questions about retrieved sets and order.

Try to reason through the mechanism

  1. Identify what the system is trying to preserve or measure in the situation.
  2. Separate the observed symptom from the cause described in this lesson.
  3. Choose the operation, model or trade-off that fits that cause; name its failure mode.
02 / Small example

A concrete case

For a known-answer task, moving the first relevant answer from rank 10 to rank 1 raises MRR even if candidate Recall@100 is unchanged.

Equation or decision rule

P@K = hits/K; R@K = hits/total relevant; MRR = mean 1/rank_first

Know what each symbol or action means. The instructor's announced final-exam format emphasizes applying principles to short objective scenarios.

03 / Examiner's trap

A tempting answer that fails

Using MRR to measure how many relevant documents were recovered.

Use an explicit four-part answer: symptom → principle → mechanism → consequence. A strong answer explains why the apparently easy choice is unsafe or incomplete.

04 / Active recall

Exam-style scenario

Two systems contain exactly the same relevant documents in top 100, but one puts the first hit at rank 1 rather than 10. Which metric necessarily improves?
One-line viva / short-answer question

How does nDCG differ from Recall@K?

nDCG evaluates graded relevance and position; Recall@K measures coverage by the cutoff.
