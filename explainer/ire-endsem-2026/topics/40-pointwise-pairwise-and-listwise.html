Lesson 40 / 69 · Ranking & signals

Pointwise, pairwise and listwise

The training loss may inspect one item, two compared items or a whole result list.

High yieldProvided module reference

Basis: M3 p.18. Page labels point to the provided files or named reading; the explanation and exercises here are independently written.

01 / Understand

What actually happens?

Pointwise predicts per-document labels; pairwise teaches ordering of preferred items; listwise approaches prioritize full-list quality. LambdaRank uses pairwise gradients weighted by their change in a ranking metric.

Core principle
The training loss may inspect one item, two compared items or a whole result list.

Try to reason through the mechanism

  1. Identify what the system is trying to preserve or measure in the situation.
  2. Separate the observed symptom from the cause described in this lesson.
  3. Choose the operation, model or trade-off that fits that cause; name its failure mode.
02 / Small example

A concrete case

Swapping ranks 1 and 2 matters far more to nDCG than swapping 99 and 100; plain RankNet treats pairs too similarly.

Equation or decision rule

RankNet loss = −log σ(score_i−score_j) for preferred i

Know what each symbol or action means. The instructor's announced final-exam format emphasizes applying principles to short objective scenarios.

03 / Examiner's trap

A tempting answer that fails

Assuming every pairwise ranking error has equal user impact.

Use an explicit four-part answer: symptom → principle → mechanism → consequence. A strong answer explains why the apparently easy choice is unsafe or incomplete.

04 / Active recall

Exam-style scenario

Which training idea weights pairs by the nDCG change caused by swapping them?
One-line viva / short-answer question

Give the central weakness of pointwise ranking.

It ignores that quality is relative within the query's candidate list.
