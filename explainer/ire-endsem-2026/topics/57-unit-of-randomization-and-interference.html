Lesson 57 / 69 · A/B testing

Unit of randomization and interference

Randomize the unit that can receive treatment without leaking effects across groups.

ExtensionSyllabus-derived extension

Basis: End-sem announcement 14 Nov; A/B testing extension. Page labels point to the provided files or named reading; the explanation and exercises here are independently written.

01 / Understand

What actually happens?

User-level assignment often prevents one person from seeing inconsistent variants. If one user's experience changes another user's outcome, the stable-unit-treatment-value assumption can fail; clusters such as markets may be required.

Core principle
Randomize the unit that can receive treatment without leaking effects across groups.

Try to reason through the mechanism

  1. Identify what the system is trying to preserve or measure in the situation.
  2. Separate the observed symptom from the cause described in this lesson.

03 / Examiner's trap

A tempting answer that fails

Assuming request-level randomization is always safe in persistent products.

Use an explicit four-part answer: symptom → principle → mechanism → consequence. A strong answer explains why the apparently easy choice is unsafe or incomplete.

04 / Active recall

Exam-style scenario

Users see alternating UI variants on successive requests and learn from both. Which change helps?
One-line viva / short-answer question

Give one interference example.

Treatment users publish content that control users consume, affecting the controls' outcomes.
