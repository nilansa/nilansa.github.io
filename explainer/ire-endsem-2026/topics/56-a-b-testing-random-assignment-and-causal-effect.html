A/B testing: random assignment and causal effect

Random assignment separates a product change's effect from pre-existing user differences.

High yieldSyllabus-derived extension

Basis: End-sem announcement 14 Nov; A/B testing extension. Page labels point to the provided files or named reading; the explanation and exercises here are independently written.

01 / Understand

What actually happens?

Split eligible units into control and treatment before outcomes occur, expose them consistently, and compare outcomes on an agreed metric. If high-intent users disproportionately choose treatment, the measured difference may be selection rather than the feature. Randomization is what supports a causal interpretation.

Core principle
Random assignment separates a product change's effect from pre-existing user differences.

Try to reason through the mechanism

  1. Identify what the system is trying to preserve or measure in the situation.
  2. Separate the observed symptom from the cause described in this lesson.

02 / Small example

A concrete case

A new search ranking is enabled at random for half of users, with the original ranker for the other half.

Equation or decision rule

estimated effect = mean(metric|T)−mean(metric|C)

03 / Examiner's trap

A tempting answer that fails

Comparing self-selected beta users with all ordinary users.

Use an explicit four-part answer: symptom → principle → mechanism → consequence. A strong answer explains why the apparently easy choice is unsafe or incomplete.

04 / Active recall

Exam-style scenario

An experimental feature is only used by motivated volunteers and shows higher CTR. Can this prove causal benefit?
One-line viva / short-answer question

What makes an A/B estimate causal?

Proper random assignment, well-defined treatment, valid units and trustworthy outcomes.
