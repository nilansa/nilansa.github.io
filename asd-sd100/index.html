PROJECT 1

Feedback-Budgeted Retriever Learning

What is a retriever? Given a question, it chooses relevant passages from a large corpus. A bi-encoder rapidly matches learned query and document vectors; a cross-encoder can examine a query–passage pair more carefully but is costly.

SciFact query→Candidate passages→Teacher scores / gold labels→Train retriever→nDCG@10

Dataset: SciFact is scientific claim/evidence retrieval, not a purely biomedical corpus. MedCPT is a biomedical-domain teacher tried as a stronger scorer; its domain choice does not make SciFact biomedical. The pilot has 505 training queries (405 TRAIN′ + 100 INNER development), a 200-query held-out set already accessed once, and 5,183 corpus documents in the C5 protocol.

Observed

Gold-only post-training improved nDCG@10 by +0.058 [0.033, 0.084]. The original MiniLM-teacher distillation hurt gold-only by −0.017 [−0.034, −0.001].

MedCPT teacher improved training-query reranking by +0.067 over MiniLM, but student transfer on INNER was only +0.0065 [−0.013, +0.027].

C5 full-encoder gate completed — FAIL

C5 measured: the three-seed full bi-encoder trial achieved KD − gold +0.005255 nDCG@10 (paired 95% CI [−0.008486,+0.018968], 200 queries). All seed effects were positive, but the gain missed +0.010 and the confidence interval crossed zero. Frozen G5 verdict: FAIL.

Failure means no-go for this particular labeled KD framing; it does not automatically reject teacher-only label-scarce learning.

PROJECT 5

Minimal Sufficient Evidence Retrieval

The distinction: a document can be relevant but insufficient. A two-hop question may need both passages. We score whether the first two retrieved paragraphs contain the entire gold evidence set (FS@2), not whether either one looks semantically similar.

Multi-hop question→Passage A + passage B→Complete gold support?→Train feedback-selection policy

Dataset limitation: 600 HotpotQA questions (200 DEV / 400 pilot holdout), only 10 supplied candidate paragraphs per question. The 400-question holdout has already been analyzed. This is a closed-candidate diagnostic, not realistic open-corpus retrieval.

Measured exploratory effects

DISCo-style approximation: FS@2 of 0.352 versus dense 0.272 in the bridge pilot. Set-conditioning improvement over independent selection: +0.028 [−0.049, +0.083], inconclusive.

DEV entity links: +0.038 [+0.013, +0.069] on 159 bridge questions, but comparison questions became substantially worse. The cross-encoder conditioning contrast was +0.050 [−0.019, +0.113], inconclusive.

Why the research pivot?

DISCo, SetR, Sufficient Context and HALT already study set retrieval, evidence sufficiency and stopping. SCARLet already trains retrievers using shared-context passage utility. PRD already economizes ranking-teacher queries.

Remaining hypothesis: under identical total teacher input/output tokens, pick feedback examples near an evidence-set sufficiency boundary instead of uniformly or by ranking alone, improving complete-support recall by ≥3 percentage points on independent evaluation.

New result · B2 evidence-sufficiency detector

The completed B2 experiment used 200 development questions (159 bridge and 41 comparison), with separate training-pool questions and no scoring of its 400-question holdout. It tested a cheap logistic classifier against a fine-tuned cross-encoder for deciding whether enough gold-support passages had arrived.

B4 dataset-label audit (200 DEV questions): 57/200 (28.5%, 95% CI 22.5–35.0%) have the answer phrase in a non-gold paragraph; 100/200 (50%) have a non-gold paragraph with a gold-title overlap. These are lexical redundancy indicators, not evidence of semantic sufficiency. They weaken a claim that the supplied two-paragraph gold set is the unique minimal answer-supporting set.

New result · B3R teacher-feedback allocation (completed DEV pilot)

Fixed pilot: 1,000 training questions sampled from a 6,742-question training pool, 200 DEV questions, three training seeds, frozen MiniLM with a small trainable query adapter, ten supplied passages per question. A total of 9,857 distinct teacher query–passage endpoints were physically scored across the shared experiment; all five scoring checkpoints and 17 artifact hashes verified. The original 400-question pilot holdout was not evaluated again.

PROFESSOR BRIEFING

Defensible conclusions and next experiments

Motivation: retriever post-training lacks dense gold relevance labels; strong feedback (cross-encoders/LLMs) is expensive. We want better retrieval per unit of teacher feedback.
First surprising finding: gold-only training helps SciFact, but original KD was harmful and the completed three-seed full-encoder KD trial failed the predefined gate (+0.0053, CI crosses zero). Better teacher ranking ≠ better trained student.
Second finding: multi-hop retrieval needs complementary paragraphs. We see a pilot gain and an entity-graph signal, but set selection alone is not new, and performance varies by question type.
New falsification: the completed B3R DEV pilot finds set-aware feedback selection below random at 2 and 5 pair labels/question. The more ambitious boundary-aware acquisition idea remains a hypothesis; a token-matched, stronger-student, independent-holdout experiment is necessary before a novelty claim.
Falsification plan: freeze treatment, use truly independent holdout questions, then evaluate SciFact and additional retrieval domains; for P5, move beyond 10 supplied paragraphs into open-corpus HotpotQA and transfer to 2Wiki/MuSiQue.

Questions you may be asked

Why use a biomedical reranker for SciFact?

MedCPT was tested as a stronger scorer, not because SciFact is exclusively biomedical. It improved teacher-side reranking but did not establish improved student retrieval. The model-domain mismatch and loss calibration are possible explanations, not proven causes.

Why isn't +0.0065 a success?

The 95% interval [−0.013, +0.027] includes zero and the improvement missed the predefined meaningful-effect gate. It could be noise, or a small effect too weak to establish with this sample.

What did the completed C5 gate show?

Full-encoder KD beat full-encoder gold-only by +0.005255 nDCG@10 [−0.008486,+0.018968] on 200 previously accessed SciFact holdout queries, across three seeds. All seeds were positive, but the predefined gain threshold was +0.010 and the paired CI lower bound had to be positive. Both failed. This is a measured negative finding, not a universal proof that distillation is useless.

What exactly is the proposed novelty?

Not distillation or set retrieval themselves. The narrow candidate is feedback acquisition before paying the teacher, based on marginal complementary evidence under a fixed teacher-token budget, that then improves a trained dense retriever against close baselines.

Did B3R feedback allocation beat random?

No. On 200 DEV questions, set-aware minus uniform FS@2 was −0.025 [−0.058, +0.010] at 2 pair labels/question, and −0.027 [−0.057, −0.000] at 5. The second comparison used about 29% more teacher input tokens for set-aware, so neither supports a cost-efficiency gain. The cross-domain, open-pool and independent-holdout gates remain unmet.

Have we shown open-corpus gains or A*-level evidence?

No. P5 has a closed 10-candidate pilot and P1 is mostly one small corpus. Several results were selected on the same development queries. Cross-domain confirmatory testing remains necessary.

How do we explain negative findings constructively?

They identify necessary controls: isolate stronger teacher quality from student learning, isolate set-conditioning from cross-encoder strength, and isolate acquisition policy from feedback format and additional cost. This is a sharper experimental research problem than the initial framing.
