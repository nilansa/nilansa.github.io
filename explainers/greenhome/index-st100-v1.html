The thesis, in 30 seconds

GreenHome turns incomplete UK home-energy evidence into a cautious retrofit decision. It distinguishes recorded facts from unknowns, scores seven dimensions, rules out unsuitable improvements, compares interacting upgrades and explains the results. Two trained models estimate electricity or gas use only when readings are missing. A local LLM interprets language; it never invents a score or financial number.

“We do not recommend every green upgrade. We show which upgrades this home can actually consider, what must be checked first, and how much the result depends on assumptions.”

One system. Five responsibilities.

1. EvidenceReal anonymised NEED readings + homeowner-confirmed conditions
→
2. Optional MLTwo regressors fill missing energy readings—not retrofit effects
→
3. Deterministic engineScore · feasibility · packages · electricity/gas · carbon/bills
→
4. Bounded LLMParse intentions; select approved fact IDs
→
5. CustomerDecision, trade-offs, evidence, checks still needed
Important nuance: the ML predictor is an optional side branch, not a mandatory stage of every request. With valid meter readings, the numerical engine uses those readings without calling ML. The assistant is also optional; the core calculator runs without Ollama.

The actual model inventory

QuestionWhat runsWhat must not be claimed
How much energy does this house use?Recorded 2024 fuel readings; if absent, optional two fitted scikit-learn regressors“We trained a retrofit-outcome model.”
How green is it?Seven weighted, monotonic subscore mappings in src/engine.js“The score is a new EPC certificate.”
Which upgrades can be considered?Five hard-coded measures with evidence-based prerequisites and blocking rules“An ML classifier learned installer suitability.”
What happens if I choose upgrades?Sequential deterministic energy simulator, then emissions and variable-bill arithmetic“These are measured causal savings.”
Will my property value increase?Restricted EPC association-based sensitivity from Nationwide, with explicit inputs“We predict sale price or guarantee uplift.”
What does the AI do?Local Qwen3:4B or DeepSeek fallback selects structured inputs/fact IDs“We use Gemma or an unrestricted numerical LLM.”

Modelled result

About 13.6% less purchased energy, 755 kg CO₂e/year lower estimated emissions, and £330/year lower variable bills. Not measured outcomes or guaranteed savings.

Why this is the right demonstration

The original dataset does not tell us whether the roof is sound or whether the loft has adequate insulation. A responsible system asks, rather than silently inferring. The demo should show one unknown becoming a confirmed input; only then does the set of feasible measures change.

2

Explain the actual training

Two fuel models, five source categories, 60/20/20, absolute-error boosting, baseline and uncertainty.

3

Explain one exact calculation

Pass from recorded energy to a feasible plan; show why heat pumps can raise electricity.

4

Explain the strict AI boundary

Natural language → validated JSON → user confirmation → model rerun → selected grounded facts.

5

Rehearse the four-minute story

One property, one business decision, one reliability check, one memorable closing statement.

6

Defend assumptions and weaknesses

Answer honestly where the prototype is a screening model rather than a field-validated product.
