Chapter 03 / Scoring & simulation

The calculation engine

Weighted score, feasibility, fuel-aware scenario arithmetic and budget-aware plan selection.

Based on actual submitted codeNo invented model capabilitiesOffline reading

First: a transparent Green Home Score

The score is not produced by ML. It is a fixed weighted sum of seven ordered indicators. Each indicator is mapped to 0–100, then multiplied by Lloyds' mandated weight.

S = w₁·s₁ + w₂·s₂ + … + w₇·s₇

Read it: S is the reported home score, rounded to an integer. Each s is one component's 0–100 subscore, and each w is its fixed fraction of the total. The weights add up to 1. The mapping must be monotonic: improving one component must not lower that component's score.

Energy efficiency (EPC)25%

Bars show relative importance only. Values are fixed in SCORE_WEIGHTS, not learned from data.

Energy-intensity subscore = clamp[100 − ((annual kWh / area m²) − 75) × 100 / 275, 0, 100]

Read it: divide annual purchased electricity-plus-gas by floor area to obtain kWh per square metre; translate that intensity onto a declining 0–100 scale. “Clamp” means values below 0 become 0 and above 100 become 100. The constants 75 and 275 are a documented scoring convention, not fitted physical parameters.

Worked interaction: loft + controls
Gas saved = (24,700 × 0.80) × [1 − (0.85 × 0.93)] = 4,139.7 kWh/yr

Read it: assume 80% of the gas was used for space heating. Loft insulation leaves 85% of that heating demand; smart controls then leave 93% of the remaining heating demand. The savings are not simply 15% + 7% of the whole gas bill.

Judge-ready explanation: “The total-energy graph alone could be misleading. We show gas and grid electricity separately. With the heat pump, electricity rises while gas drops. We then recompute carbon using each fuel's own factor; we never multiply total saved kWh by a single blanket carbon factor.”

Carbon and bills: simple formulas, precise scope

ΔCO₂e = (E₀ − E₁) × 0.13096 + (G₀ − G₁) × 0.18231

Read it: E₀ and G₀ are baseline annual grid electricity and gas, each in kWh. E₁ and G₁ are the post-scenario fuel values. The differences are savings (negative if a fuel increases). Multiply by the 2026 UK emissions factor for that fuel, in kg CO₂e per kWh, and add. The result is estimated kg CO₂e saved per year.

Annual variable-bill saving = (E₀ − E₁) × £0.2632 + (G₀ − G₁) × £0.0797

Those are published October–December 2026 Ofgem average unit-rate inputs. They are not personalised tariffs. Export revenue, standing charges, price changes, maintenance and financing are excluded.

Fourth: select a package, don't just list improvements

With up to five measures, the planner enumerates all possible subsets: at most 2⁵ = 32, including a no-work baseline. It rejects blocked work, requires opt-in for conditional work, and filters by a user-selected budget if present. Depending on the objective, it ranks by purchased kWh saved, estimated CO₂e saved, variable-bill saving, or kWh reduction per known upfront pound; the UI also surfaces a low-upfront-cost comparison.

Example: for the NH-083 corrected scenario with a £2,000 cap and the objective of reducing bills, loft insulation plus smart controls is the best-ranked feasible package: £1,450, 4,139.7 kWh/year modelled savings and £330/year variable-bill savings. A more expensive option may save more energy but is outside this customer's cap.

Property value is a separate, restricted sensitivity—not a prediction

Runtime method

Nationwide published EPC associations

For an owner-occupied home in England, a user supplies the current price plus explicit before/after EPC groups. The module varies the reported association from zero to its published magnitude. It supports group comparisons involving A/B, D and F/G.

What stays unavailable

No automatic uplift from a retrofit

The app does not predict a new EPC, individual sale price or causal retrofit-value effect. Nationwide did not publish numerical C/E coefficients for this implementation, so C/E transitions are unsupported.

The separately fitted linked-sales regression had weak causal interpretation and contradictory EPC associations in its restricted sample; it is documented as a diagnostic and not used for runtime valuations.
