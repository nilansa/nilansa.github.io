Chapter 06 / Judge questions

Defend every claim

Precise answers to likely technical, scientific, commercial and trust questions.

Based on actual submitted codeNo invented model capabilitiesOffline reading

How to answer well

Give the answer in three parts: what the code does, what evidence supports it, and what remains unverified. Avoid claiming that a rule-based simulator is a fitted causal predictor. Expand a question below and practise speaking its answer in 15–25 seconds.

Business value and problem definition

1. What problem is GreenHome solving?
Homeowners face an ordering and confidence problem: not every retrofit suits every property, and headline savings can be misleading. GreenHome combines energy evidence, constraints, plausible upgrade sequences, budget choices and explicit caveats into a transparent screening plan. It does not replace installers or retrofit surveys.
2. Who exactly is the target customer?
The prototype is oriented to homeowners considering efficiency improvements in England and Wales, based on NEED source coverage. Its separate property-value sensitivity only supports owner-occupied homes in England. No Lloyds customer data, account integration or actual homeowner study is included.
3. How is this different from Lloyds' existing home-energy improvement tool?
Our differentiators are a visible seven-part provisional score, explicit feasible-versus-conditional screening, plan comparisons with interacting fuel changes and evidence provenance. The public Lloyds tool also offers personalised plans. We did not establish better prediction accuracy or conversion than that commercial tool, because we could not perform consented same-home comparisons.
4. What does “green” actually mean here?
Our green screening focuses on energy efficiency, annual purchased electricity and gas, and operational emissions. It does not claim a whole-building lifecycle assessment: embodied carbon, construction waste, future weather, biodiversity and comfort outcomes are outside the quantified model.

Data and modelling

5. Which dataset did you train on? Was it synthetic?
We used the genuine DESNZ NEED 2026 anonymised 50,000-row sample with 2024 annual energy observations. The 100-home UI sample contains distinct source rows, deliberately balanced 20 per EPC group; it is not the model's training set. Counterfactual retrofit outcomes are assumptions in a simulator, not observed labels or synthetic claims of real-world accuracy.
6. Why only five features?
These are five common NEED categorical descriptors available for training and inference: property type, age band, floor-area band, region and main heating fuel. We deliberately omit EPC as a fuel-model feature because its timing relative to 2024 usage is uncertain. We lack occupancy, exact heating system, condition and tariff labels; they would be valuable future inputs with correctly dated observations.
7. Which trained model did you choose and why?
Two independent scikit-learn histogram gradient-boosting regressors with loss="absolute_error", one for electricity and one for gas. We compared a grouped-median baseline and squared- versus absolute-error boosting on the validation partition. The absolute-error candidate had the best validation MAE for each fuel.
8. What exactly are the held-out results?
Electricity MAE was 1,612 kWh/year versus 1,649 for a grouped-median baseline—about 2.2% lower. Gas MAE was 4,053 versus 4,312—about 6.0% lower. These are improvements against a small baseline, not proof that a single property's consumption is accurately known.
9. How do you avoid train–test leakage?
The training script splits source rows 60/20/20 once with seed 42, fits one-hot categories on training rows only, selects candidate models by validation MAE and scores test after selection. There is still a stated temporal risk: source property attributes are not all independently dated as of 2024. We disclose that rather than claiming perfect leakage freedom.
10. Why not train an LLM or deep neural network for recommendations?
The sponsor requires reproducible, explainable and feasible outputs. We did not have real, linked before/after retrofit labels for supervised retrofit training. Fitting a powerful model to simulated savings would only learn our assumptions. We use rules and deterministic scenario calculations for the limited evidence available, and train tabular models only where actual meter targets exist.
11. How fast can you retrain the energy predictors?
The recorded CPU training run took 4.173 seconds from local CSV load through model serialization on two threads; model artifacts total about 401 KB. Downloading the public source, installing dependencies and validating a changed feature set require additional time. This timing is a measured run, not a guaranteed SLA.
12. What happens when an input is unknown?
Missing fuel readings stay unavailable unless the customer requests a separate model estimate. Missing roofing, planning or electrical status yields a conditional check rather than an assumed pass. Unknown scoring components use a neutral convention with a provisional warning and sensitivity range. Zero and unknown are kept distinct.

Score, recommendations and carbon

13. What is the score formula?
A fixed sum of seven component subscores, each 0–100, with weights 25%, 20%, 15%, 15%, 10%, 10% and 5% for EPC efficiency, purchased energy intensity, insulation, heating, glazing, renewables and smart management. The code rounds the weighted result and maps it to Lloyds' five classes. The mappings are monotonic and documented; it is not a certified EPC rating.
14. Why can a poor EPC home have low energy use?
Purchased use depends on occupancy and heating behavior as well as fabric. A home can have low bills because it is underheated, which is a customer-welfare concern. The tool acknowledges this and cannot certify comfort or technical thermal performance from the energy record alone.
15. How are retrofits ranked and filtered?
Each of five measures passes explicit applicability rules. The engine classifies it as feasible, needs assessment or not suitable, then enumerates at most 32 package subsets. It excludes blocked upgrades and unknown-cost plans under a budget, and can optimise energy kWh, carbon, variable bills or energy savings per known pound. Conditional work requires explicit opt-in.
16. Why must insulation come before a heat pump?
Fabric measures reduce assumed space-heating demand before the heat-pump conversion, so the scenario avoids simply adding independent percentages. That is a modelling and good-practice ordering preference; the implementation does not treat insulation as a universal hard prerequisite for a heat pump. A real installer must perform a heat-loss calculation and design.
17. Can a heat pump increase electricity while lowering total energy?
Yes. The engine removes an assumed gas-heating demand and adds electric demand scaled by the old boiler's 85% efficiency divided by a COP of 3. The source gas use falls; electricity can rise. The carbon and bill calculations respect both signed fuel deltas, rather than hiding the increase in an aggregate number.
18. How does solar work in your calculations?
The submitted scenario assumes 3 kWp of panels, 850 kWh per kWp yearly yield and 40% self-use: up to 1,020 kWh/year of grid import is offset, bounded by the home's post-retrofit electricity demand. The model does not reduce underlying demand or estimate export income.
19. Where do carbon savings come from?
We calculate electricity savings times the configured 2026 UK electricity factor (0.13096 kg CO₂e/kWh), plus gas savings times its factor (0.18231 kg CO₂e/kWh). A negative saving reduces the net reduction. These are operational-use factors, not lifecycle emissions.
20. What about installation costs and return on investment?
The five measures use documented, indicative upfront costs. Variable-bill savings use configured Ofgem average unit rates; simple payback divides known upfront cost by positive annual variable-bill savings. It excludes finance, standing charges, grants, maintenance and tariff changes. It is an early screening comparison, not investment advice.

Property values and causality

21. How do you predict property-value uplift?
We do not predict a causal uplift or new sale price. A separate module offers an optional sensitivity for a user-supplied price and explicit EPC transition, limited to Nationwide's reported associations for owner-occupied England homes among A/B, D and F/G groups. C/E transitions are unavailable because the source did not quantify them.
22. But you trained a property-price regression—why not use it?
The diagnostic on 9,954 linked transaction records gave strong within-sample explanatory fit but a £74,642 holdout price MAE, and produced EPC associations with counterintuitive directions relative to the published national analysis. Certificate timing also risks retrospective leakage. The team correctly kept it as a diagnostic, not an operational valuation.

LLM architecture and trust

23. What exactly does Qwen do?
It parses customer text into an approved JSON schema—current facts, requested actions, objective, optional budget—or selects a bounded list of fact IDs for an answer. Server code validates the proposed fields, asks for confirmation, reruns the numerical engine and renders the facts. It is not allowed to calculate scores, money or emissions.
24. What if the model hallucinates or goes offline?
The model cannot add unsupported field identifiers, invented numerical values or unrestricted explanation text. Grounding checks reject unsupported parse values, and failed or absent local inference falls back to narrow deterministic rules. The score and simulator continue without the LLM. These safeguards reduce risk but do not prove zero misunderstandings.
25. Did you fine-tune Qwen or use Gemma?
No. The default text model is the pre-existing local qwen3:4b tag; deepseek-r1:7b is the fallback. Both run via Ollama when installed. No local LLM fine-tuning code exists. The only team-trained runtime models are the two scikit-learn NEED consumption regressors.
26. Can it infer roof damage from photographs?
The app can preview a photo. An optional vision pathway is constrained to visible clues, and any suggestion must be confirmed. The recorded final test environment had no installed compatible vision model, so we cannot claim live vision inference passed. Neither a photo nor text can certify moisture, hidden insulation or structural soundness.
27. How did you evaluate the language model?
A local Qwen run matched 13/13 authored synthetic parse cases, including negation, budgets, uncertainty and prompt-like instructions, with automated groundedness checks; the median request took about 3.8 seconds. These cases are not a representative, human-reviewed groundedness or customer-comprehension evaluation. A larger held-out natural-query set is future work.

Production and responsible use

28. Could this support millions of customers today?
Not as deployed. The numerical engine is small and easy to replicate, but the local single-process Ollama queue, localhost-only server, absence of authentication, lack of audited customer data access and no operational monitoring make this a prototype. Production would require managed service separation, versioned datasets, privacy review, access control, deployment tests and evaluated latency/cost.
29. What is the strongest engineering achievement?
Consistent trust boundaries: observed energy is distinguished from missing or estimated, infeasible measures are blocked, interacting fuel changes are calculated reproducibly, and the LLM cannot invent numbers. The repo also contains documented tests and explicit model evidence. It is more than a static design prototype.
30. What is the biggest scientific limitation?
We have no observed same-home post-retrofit outcomes for this sample. The scenario simulator uses plausible assumptions, not field-validated causal savings. The property-value module is not a causal predictor either. Those two gaps are first priorities before real-world decision support.
31. How do you protect customers?
The demo is an independent local public prototype with anonymised source homes and session-only edits. An optional private-home memory is browser-local and consent-based; photos are not stored as customer profiles. It does not claim lending eligibility, installer approval, grant approval or certified property valuations. Production security would need substantially more controls.
32. What would you build next?
First validate savings against consenting homes with dated meter, EPC, installation and survey records; calibrate uncertainty and financial cases. Then add time-aligned property features and retrain the consumption models with an untouched test set. Finally evaluate human understanding and the LLM against real, held-out questions before wider rollout. See the prioritised roadmap.

Three difficult answers to rehearse perfectly

“Is this really AI?”

“Yes, for missing energy prediction and language understanding. The recommendation arithmetic is intentionally deterministic because the available data does not justify learned retrofit effects.”

“Can I trust the savings?”

“As transparent scenario estimates—not guaranteed outcomes. We show assumptions, separate both fuels, and require site checks before a customer acts.”

“What would Lloyds gain?”

“A clearer screening and education journey: prioritise plausible actions, expose unknowns, reduce inappropriate recommendations and identify where a customer needs an assessment.”
